# Improving Prophet Accuracy on Intraday Futures (NQ 4h Close) — Research Report

> Compiled from official Prophet docs, the facebook/prophet GitHub issue tracker, Hyndman's FPP3, NeuralProphet docs, and practitioner critiques (Manokhin, Advancing Analytics, Bytepawn, Journey Further). All URLs cited inline. Used as the basis for the design in `03_design.md`.

## Headline conclusions (what actually moves RMSE)

- **Target transformation matters more than any Prophet knob.** Forecast log-returns or first-differences and reconstruct price; modelling raw price means you're modelling a near-random walk with a smooth trend that Prophet was not designed for ([Hyndman, FPP3 §12.2](https://otexts.com/fpp3/prophet.html); [Manokhin, "Snake Oil of Prophet"](https://valeman.medium.com/the-snake-oil-of-applying-facebook-prophet-to-financial-time-series-0a7e2814a614)).
- **`changepoint_prior_scale` is the single most impactful hyperparameter** ([Prophet Diagnostics docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/diagnostics.html)). Tune it on a log scale over [0.001, 0.5] via Prophet's built-in `cross_validation` + `performance_metrics`.
- **Walk-forward / rolling-origin retraining is non-negotiable** for non-stationary series; Prophet ships it natively via `cross_validation(initial, period, horizon)` ([Diagnostics docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/diagnostics.html)).
- **`add_regressor`** with informative exogenous signals (VIX, volume, prior-bar return, session dummies) is the biggest accuracy lever beyond the target transform ([Seasonality, Holidays, Regressors docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/seasonality,_holiday_effects,_and_regressors.html)).
- **Use `growth='flat'` (or linear), never `'logistic'`** for price series — logistic growth on financial instruments is explicitly flagged as "a dangerous practice" ([Advancing Analytics, FB Prophet & the Stock Market Pt2](https://www.advancinganalytics.co.uk/blog/2021/7/26/facebook-prophet-and-the-stock-market-part-2)).
- **For sub-daily futures, restrict the future dataframe to actual session hours** — Prophet's docs warn daily seasonality is "not estimated well" when there are regular gaps ([Non-Daily Data docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/non-daily_data.html)).
- **For lag-driven series, switch to NeuralProphet.** Vanilla Prophet has no autoregressive term; NeuralProphet's AR-Net adds it explicitly ([NeuralProphet model overview](https://neuralprophet.com/science-behind/model-overview.html); [NeuralProphet paper, arXiv 2111.15397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15397)).

## Per-knob evidence

**Hyperparameter tuning.** The official Diagnostics page explicitly classifies knobs into "tune", "maybe tune", and "do not tune" buckets. Tune: `changepoint_prior_scale` ([0.001, 0.5], log-scale; "probably the most impactful"), `seasonality_prior_scale` ([0.01, 10]), `holidays_prior_scale`, and `seasonality_mode` (additive vs multiplicative). Do **not** tune: `growth`, `n_changepoints`, manual seasonalities, `mcmc_samples`, `interval_width`, `uncertainty_samples` ([Diagnostics docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/diagnostics.html)). Use Prophet's `cross_validation` + `performance_metrics` for the search; the `rolling_window` argument controls horizon granularity.

**add_regressor.** Regressors are added pre-fit; future values **must be known** for the forecast horizon, otherwise you must forecast the regressor separately (and inherit its error). Regressors can be additive or multiplicative, have their own prior scale (defaulting to holiday prior scale), and are standardised by default; constant regressors raise an error ([Seasonality/Regressors docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/seasonality,_holiday_effects,_and_regressors.html)). For NQ this means: only use regressors observable at bar-open or earlier — prior-bar return, prior-bar range, rolling volume, time-of-day dummies, day-of-week dummies, VIX previous close.

**Target transformation.** Multiple sources document log-return / first-difference transformation as standard preprocessing for financial series — log stabilises variance, differencing removes trend so the residual is closer to stationary ([Hyndman FPP3 §9.1 on stationarity/differencing](https://otexts.com/fpp3/stationarity.html); [AJTAS stationarity-in-Prophet paper](https://www.sciencepublishinggroup.com/article/10.11648/j.ajtas.20251403.12)). Prophet's docs do not officially recommend this (the model "handles non-stationary data" via its trend term), but every serious comparative study transforms before fitting and reconstructs price post-forecast.

**Walk-forward.** Prophet's `cross_validation(model, initial, period, horizon)` is exactly rolling-origin evaluation with a fixed forecast horizon and configurable cutoff spacing; `performance_metrics` then aggregates RMSE/MAE/MAPE/coverage in a rolling window over horizon distance ([Diagnostics docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/diagnostics.html)). Walk-forward retraining is the standard fix for look-ahead bias and the only reliable evaluation pattern for non-stationary financial data ([Alpha Scientist, walk-forward modelling](https://alphascientist.com/walk_forward_model_building.html)).

**Growth mode.** `linear` is the default and is correct for price-like series with no fundamental ceiling. `flat` is appropriate when forecasting a stationary target — i.e. if you've transformed to log-returns, `growth='flat'` is the right choice because returns have no long-term drift to model. `logistic` requires a `cap` (and optionally `floor`) on every row of history and future ([Saturating Forecasts docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/saturating_forecasts.html)) and is explicitly cautioned against for financial instruments ([Advancing Analytics](https://www.advancinganalytics.co.uk/blog/2021/7/26/facebook-prophet-and-the-stock-market-part-2)).

**MCMC vs MAP.** Prophet maintainer @tcuongd: "you would use MCMC sampling when you want to produce uncertainty estimates for the seasonal, holidays, and extra regressor components. In MAP estimation, we only produce uncertainties for the trend component" ([Issue #1914](https://github.com/facebook/prophet/issues/1914)). MCMC does not improve point-forecast RMSE meaningfully — it changes uncertainty quantification at ~100x runtime cost. `mcmc_samples` is on the docs' "do not tune" list ([Diagnostics docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/diagnostics.html)). Skip it for RMSE optimisation.

**NeuralProphet alternative.** NeuralProphet adds AR-Net autoregression, lagged regressors, and sparse-AR — exactly the lag structure vanilla Prophet lacks ([model overview](https://neuralprophet.com/science-behind/model-overview.html); [arXiv 2111.15397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15397)). Empirically NeuralProphet beats Prophet on short-to-medium horizons by 20-90% on MAPE in hourly-energy benchmarks, while being faster ([Bytepawn benchmark](https://bytepawn.com/comparing-neuralprophet-and-prophet-for-timeseries-forecasting.html); [Journey Further](https://www.journeyfurther.com/articles/better-forecasting-with-neuralprophet)). For 1-bar-ahead 4h NQ, the AR term is exactly what you want — the previous 4h close is the strongest predictor.

## Practitioner consensus on Prophet-for-stocks (no sugar-coating)

The consensus, including from Prophet's own maintainers, is that **Prophet is the wrong tool for price-level forecasting of liquid financial instruments**. Prophet maintainer @bletham on Issue #1502: *"Prophet is best-suited for time series with the sort of seasonal trends that are typical to business time series. I'm doubtful that stock price data has the sort of seasonal structure that would make Prophet a good choice"* ([Issue #1502](https://github.com/facebook/prophet/issues/1502)). Hyndman in FPP3: *"it rarely gives better forecast accuracy than the alternative approaches"* ([FPP3 §12.2](https://otexts.com/fpp3/prophet.html)). Manokhin's widely-cited critique argues Prophet ignores volatility clustering and fat tails, has no autoregressive lags, and confuses noise for trend via the Slutsky effect ([Snake Oil article](https://valeman.medium.com/the-snake-oil-of-applying-facebook-prophet-to-financial-time-series-0a7e2814a614)). Comparative studies typically show ARIMA/SARIMAX beating Prophet on financial RMSE (e.g. RMSE 24.4 vs 37.5; SARIMAX 8% vs Prophet 11.4% on financial benchmarks reported in [Adebayo et al., SSRN 5206738](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5206738) and related comparative papers).

## Specific actionable recommendations for NQ 4h close

1. **Transform the target.** Fit Prophet on `log_return = log(close_t / close_{t-1})`, not on raw close. Reconstruct price post-forecast by cumulative product. Set `growth='flat'` on the transformed target.
2. **Restrict to active session hours.** Build the future dataframe only across hours that exist in history (CME globex schedule) — per [Non-Daily Data docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/non-daily_data.html), Prophet's daily seasonality is unconstrained where data is absent.
3. **Hyperparameter grid (log-scale, 4–5 points each):** `changepoint_prior_scale ∈ [0.001, 0.5]`, `seasonality_prior_scale ∈ [0.01, 10]`, `seasonality_mode ∈ {additive, multiplicative}`. Evaluate via `cross_validation(initial='180 days', period='14 days', horizon='4 hours')` with `performance_metrics` reporting RMSE ([Diagnostics docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/diagnostics.html)).
4. **Add regressors known at bar-open:** prior-bar log-return, prior-bar realised range, rolling 20-bar volatility, time-of-day one-hot (RTH open/lunch/close, Asia, EU), day-of-week one-hot, VIX prior close. All standardised (Prophet default).
5. **Custom seasonalities:** add an explicit 6-bar (daily) and 30-bar (weekly) Fourier seasonality with modest `fourier_order` (3–5) instead of relying on `daily_seasonality=True` which assumes 24-hour-continuous data.
6. **Walk-forward retraining cadence:** retrain every N bars (weekly is typical for 4h data) rather than once globally.
7. **Benchmark against:** (a) naive — predict next return = 0, i.e. price = previous close; (b) ARIMA(1,0,1) on returns; (c) NeuralProphet with `n_lags=10..20`, `n_forecasts=1`. If Prophet does not beat the naive baseline on RMSE, it is contributing zero alpha.

## Known dead-ends — don't waste cycles

- **MCMC sampling** for RMSE improvement. Maintainer confirms it only changes uncertainty intervals, not point forecasts ([Issue #1914](https://github.com/facebook/prophet/issues/1914)); docs explicitly say don't tune it.
- **Logistic growth with a price cap.** Cautioned against for financial instruments ([Advancing Analytics](https://www.advancinganalytics.co.uk/blog/2021/7/26/facebook-prophet-and-the-stock-market-part-2)).
- **`changepoint_range`** — docs note it's hard to tune via CV ([Diagnostics docs](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/diagnostics.html)).
- **Manual `n_changepoints` / `changepoints`** — on the "do not tune" list; rely on `changepoint_prior_scale` instead.
- **Holidays for futures.** Equity-index futures trade nearly 24/5; the US holiday calendar produces zeros, not informative gaps. If used at all, use as session-closed dummies, not as holiday effects.
- **Forecasting raw close prices and reading patterns off the trend.** This is the textbook Slutsky-effect failure mode ([Manokhin](https://valeman.medium.com/the-snake-oil-of-applying-facebook-prophet-to-financial-time-series-0a7e2814a614)).
- **Forecasting horizons longer than ~1 bar** for evaluating the model. The non-stationary structure dominates beyond ~1 step; either retrain more often or accept that horizon-2+ RMSE is dominated by drift, not model skill.

## Bottom line

For a 1-bar-ahead 4h NQ close, the realistic ceiling on Prophet's RMSE improvement comes from: (1) switching the target to log-returns, (2) adding `add_regressor` features computable at bar-open, (3) running proper walk-forward CV with `changepoint_prior_scale` tuned on a log scale, (4) restricting future timestamps to actual session hours. If after this you still cannot beat a naive previous-close baseline, the correct next step is NeuralProphet with AR-Net (or ARIMA/SARIMAX on returns) — not more Prophet tuning. Prophet's own maintainers and the broader forecasting community agree the model's structural assumptions (smooth piecewise-linear trend + Fourier seasonality + no autoregression) are a poor fit for liquid futures price series.
