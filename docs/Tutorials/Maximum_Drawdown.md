Maximum Drawdown (Max DD) measures the largest peak-to-trough decline in the value of a stock, portfolio, or trading account before a new peak is attained. It quantifies the absolute worst-case scenario or maximum downside risk an investor would have experienced over a specific time period.
## How it Works
Max Drawdown focuses strictly on capital preservation by looking at the largest drop from a high point to the subsequent low point.

* Formula: $\text{Max Drawdown} = \frac{\text{Peak Value} - \text{Trough Value}}{\text{Peak Value}} \times 100$
* Example: If your trading account grows from $\$10,000$ to a peak of $\$15,000$, drops down to $\$9,000$, and then rallies back up to $\$16,000$, your Max Drawdown is calculated from the $\$15,000$ peak to the $\$9,000$ trough. This represents a 40% Max Drawdown $(\frac{15,000 - 9,000}{15,000})$.

## The Psychological and Recovery Trap
Large drawdowns are mathematically dangerous because the return required to recover grows exponentially as the drawdown deepens.

* A 10% drawdown requires an 11% gain to break even.
* A 25% drawdown requires a 33% gain to break even.
* A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain just to get back to your original peak.

## Connecting the Metrics
Max Drawdown ties together all the trading concepts discussed so far:

* The Cause (Bad RSI & SL Execution): If you ignore an RSI Overbought signal, skip setting a Stop-Loss (SL), or utilize a poor P/L Ratio, a single losing trade can spiral out of control and trigger a massive Max Drawdown.
* The Relationship to Sharpe Ratio: The Sharpe Ratio punishes high volatility. A portfolio with a high Max Drawdown will inherently have a severely damaged Sharpe Ratio because the equity growth curve is unstable.
