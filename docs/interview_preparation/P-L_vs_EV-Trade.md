The core difference is that P/L tells you what actually happened in the past (or on a single active trade), while EV/Trade tells you what you can statistically expect to happen on average in the future.
Here is a direct breakdown of how they differ:
## 1. Actual Performance vs. Mathematical Expectation

* P/L (Profit/Loss): This is a rearview mirror metric. It measures the absolute dollar amount or percentage you made or lost on an individual trade or across your entire account history (e.g., "I made $500 on Apple stock today").
* EV/Trade (Expected Value per Trade): This is a forward-looking statistical projection. It combines your historical win rate with your average P/L to predict how much money a single trade is worth before you even take it (e.g., "Every time I take this RSI setup, it is worth a statistical average of +$60").

## 2. The Role of Probability

* P/L ignores probability: A trade either wins or it loses. If you risk $100 to make $300 and you hit your target, your P/L for that trade is simply +$300. It doesn't factor in how likely that win was.
* EV/Trade requires probability: It relies entirely on win/loss percentages. If that $300 winning trade only happens 10% of the time, and you lose $100 the other 90% of the time, your EV/Trade is actually negative (-$60), meaning it is a bad strategy despite the high P/L potential.

## 3. Comparison Summary Table

| Feature | P/L (Profit/Loss) | EV/Trade (Expected Value) |
|---|---|---|
| What it measures | Realized or open financial gains/losses. | Long-term theoretical average per trade. |
| Data used | Final entry and exit prices. | Historical Win Rate % + Average Win/Loss size. |
| Main Use | Tracking account growth and tax tracking. | Validating if a trading strategy has a mathematical edge. |
| Focus | Individual outcomes or cumulative totals. | Long-term consistency over hundreds of trades. |

## How They Work Together
You cannot calculate EV/Trade without your historical P/L. Your past P/L gives you your average win size and average loss size. You then plug those P/L averages into the EV formula alongside your win rate to see if your trading system is mathematically viable over the long haul.
