In stock market trading, the Profit/Loss (P/L) Ratio measures your risk-to-reward by comparing your average winning trade to your average losing trade. It helps assess the effectiveness of your trading strategy and manage risk over time. [1, 2] 
## How it Works
The P/L ratio tells you how much money you stand to gain for every dollar you risk losing. [2] 

* Formula: $\text{P/L Ratio} = \frac{\text{Average Profit}}{\text{Average Loss}}$
* Example: If your average winning trade yields $\$600$ and your average losing trade costs $\$200$, your P/L ratio is $3:1$. This means you make $\$3$ in profit for every $\$1$ you lose. [1, 2, 3, 4] 

## What a Good Ratio Looks Like

* The 2:1 Benchmark: Many trading strategies aim for a P/L ratio of at least $2:1$ or $3:1$ to ensure long-term profitability. [1, 5] 
* Flexibility: A higher ratio means you can remain profitable even if you have a lower win rate. For example, a $3:1$ ratio allows you to lose over half of your trades and still break even. [2, 6] 
* Context is Key: Focusing solely on the P/L ratio can be misleading. It must be balanced with your win rate (the percentage of trades that are successful) and the statistical probability of a trade being profitable (Average Profitability Per Trade). [1, 7] 

## Common Variations

* Total P/L: The absolute dollar amount of all your cumulative profits and losses.
* P/L %: The percentage return of a specific investment relative to its initial purchase price. [2, 8, 9] 

## Confusion with P/E Ratio
Many investors confuse the P/L ratio with the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio. While P/L is a day-to-day risk management tool for traders, the P/E ratio is a fundamental analysis metric used to value a company by comparing its share price to its per-share earnings. [2, 10, 11]

In trading, TP stands for Take-Profit and SL stands for Stop-Loss. They are automated orders that manage your risk by automatically closing your position when the stock price reaches a specific level.
## Take-Profit (TP)
A Take-Profit order automatically sells a stock once it reaches a pre-set profit target.

* Purpose: It locks in your gains before the market can reverse and move against you.
* How it works: If you buy a stock at $50 and set a TP at $60, the system automatically sells it the moment the price hits $60.

## Stop-Loss (SL)
A Stop-Loss order automatically sells a stock if the price drops to a specific defense level.

* Purpose: It limits your financial loss and prevents a small mistake from ruining your account.
* How it works: If you buy a stock at $50 and set an SL at $45, the system automatically exits the trade if the price falls to $45, capping your loss at $5 per share.

## How TP and SL Create Your P/L Ratio
Your TP and SL levels directly determine your Profit/Loss ratio for any individual trade.

* Setting the ratio: If your target profit (TP) is $10 above your entry price, and your maximum risk (SL) is $5 below your entry price, you have a 2:1 P/L ratio.
* Execution: Once set, these orders run automatically, removing destructive human emotions like greed and fear from the trade.
