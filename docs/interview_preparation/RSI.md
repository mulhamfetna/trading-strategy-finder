The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a popular technical momentum indicator that measures the speed and change of a stock's price movements to identify overvalued or undervalued conditions.
## RSI Components

* RSI Period: The number of historical price bars used in the calculation, which defaults to 14 days. A shorter period makes the indicator more sensitive, while a longer period makes it smoother.
* RSI Oversold: A threshold traditionally set at 30 or below, suggesting the stock has fallen too fast and may be due for a bullish trend reversal or corrective bounce.
* RSI Overbought: A threshold traditionally set at 70 or above, suggesting the stock has risen too fast and may be due for a bearish pullback or consolidation.

## How Traders Use RSI

* Buy/Sell Signals: Traders look to buy when the RSI crosses back above 30 from oversold territory, and sell when it crosses back below 70 from overbought territory.
* Divergence: If the stock price makes a new high but the RSI fails to make a new high, it indicates weakening momentum and a potential trend reversal.
* Trend Confirmation: In a strong uptrend, RSI often hovers between 40 and 90, with the 40–50 zone acting as support. In a strong downtrend, RSI tends to stay between 10 and 60.

## Combining RSI with TP and SL
You can use RSI extremes to systematically plan your trade exits.

* Setting TP: If you are in a long position, you might set your Take-Profit (TP) order near the price level where the RSI is projected to hit 70.
* Setting SL: You can place your Stop-Loss (SL) just below the recent swing low that formed while the RSI was in the oversold zone under 30.
